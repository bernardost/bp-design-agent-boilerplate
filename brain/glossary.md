# Glossary

*The owner of **who and what the project's shorthand refers to**: people, nicknames,
acronyms, codenames, and the client's internal names for things. `tags.md` owns themes; this
owns proper nouns.*

Why it earns a file: on a design engagement the thing a new session most often lacks is not a
decision, it is knowing that "Marcy" is the VP who has to approve every screen, that "the
portal" means the logged-in dashboard and not the marketing site, and that "AF" is a person's
initials in one channel and "above the fold" in another. Left implicit, that becomes confident
invention — the failure mode the charter's label-evidence rule exists to stop.

**Keep it lean at the top.** The most-used dozen entries live under `## Hot`, which is what a
session should be able to hold in its head; everything else lives under `## Full` and may grow
forever. Promote an entry when it comes up twice; demote it when it stops. Same instinct as
`now.md`'s size limit: the useful part of a reference is the part you actually read.

**Format.** One line per entry, term first:

```
- **Marcy Okonkwo** · VP Brand, client side · approves every screen; ex-agency, reads redlines
- **the portal** · the logged-in dashboard, never the marketing site
```

**Attribution still applies.** If you inferred someone's role rather than being told it, mark
it `inferred` — a glossary is exactly where a guess hardens into a fact.
